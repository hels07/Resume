      Project 2 of 5: Connect 4 Puzzle Trainer

      My sister is in Year 9 and was chosen for Accelerated Maths. In Connect 4 she dropped discs at random, and the games online were either too simple or unexplained black boxes.

      For my IB Computer Science IA I built a trainer that drops you into a position partway through a game. You find the winning move while the AI defends with minimax search and alpha-beta pruning, then explains each move in plain English, naming every odd or even threat. All 15 puzzles are checked against Pascal Pons's exact solver.

      The board on this tile is playable. It runs a JavaScript port of the same search on your device.

      Project 3 of 5: Elo ratings in professional tennis

      My Digital Society Extended Essay asks what an Elo rating system's accuracy reveals about the limits of algorithmic fairness in tennis rankings.

      I ran Elo over 27,909 ATP matches from 2016 to 2026, using Jeff Sackmann's public match data. It picked the winner 63.5% of the time, just behind the official ATP ranking at 64.2%, and the betting market beat both at 69.4%. Elo's Brier score was 0.132, lower than the odds' 0.202 on the smaller set of 20,848 matches I could pair with betting data.

      Project 4 of 5: Canteen sales forecaster

      The canteen line was long, food kept running out, the staff were overwhelmed and people were losing their break.

      I'm building a model in Python with Facebook Prophet so the canteen can plan ahead. It turns single transactions into daily sales for each item, learns the weekly pattern, and writes seven-day forecasts for the whole canteen, each item and each hour. It's still in development and runs on simulated data while I set it up.
